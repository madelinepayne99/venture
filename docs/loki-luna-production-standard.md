# Loki & Luna — Production Quality Standard (LOCKED)

**Status: founder-set standard, binding on all future Episode 001 and
pilot-technique work.** Not a live code change — this governs judgment
calls (what counts as a PASS, what's worth pursuing further) made during
the Loki & Luna pilot, the same way `loki-luna-voice-casting.md` locks the
voice decision.

## The bar

Loki & Luna is built to a **high-end children's television standard**,
not generic AI-generated YouTube content. The audience should not be
thinking about AI while watching.

Existing successful preschool animation (referenced loosely, e.g. Bluey)
may inform high-level production *principles* — natural character acting,
readable staging, reaction timing, storytelling, sound design, editing,
emotional clarity — but nothing here may copy or imitate any protected
character, performance, script, or distinctive visual expression.

A finished episode must feel: intentionally directed, professionally
edited, visually coherent, character-led, naturally acted, emotionally
readable, properly paced, cinematic in its camera choices, clean and
consistent in character identity, professionally mixed, rich in
environmental/in-world detail, and like one continuous animated world —
never like assembled AI clips.

## Dialogue specifically

Audio laid over approximate mouth movement is not sufficient on its own.
Scenes need believable speaking performances, listening performances,
eye-lines, facial reactions, body acting, pauses, and real shot/reverse-shot
coverage where appropriate. Perfect phoneme-level lip sync is not
required stylistically, but *obviously unrelated* mouth movement is not
acceptable for production.

## How the ~£10/episode cost target gets reached

Through reusable assets, local M2 processing, smarter shot architecture,
environment reuse, deterministic composition, efficient generation,
editing, and reusable production knowledge — **never by quietly accepting
visibly inferior animation to hit the number.** If the current pipeline
genuinely cannot meet this bar, the correct response is to report the
limitation and recommend an architecture change, not lower QC silently.

## What this means for Test C specifically

Test C itself does not need to equal final broadcast quality. Its real
job is narrower: determine whether its underlying *technique* (pre-composed
start frame + locked voices + editorial sync, no native lip-sync) can
plausibly be refined into a pipeline capable of reaching the standard
above. Episode 001, not Test C, is where the actual show gets proven.
